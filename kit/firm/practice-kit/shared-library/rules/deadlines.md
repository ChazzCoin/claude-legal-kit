# Deadlines — firm rule

Always-on rule. The firm's calendar discipline is non-negotiable.

## Where deadlines live

Every matter has its own DEADLINES.md tracking all dates for that matter. The firm-level rules below govern how to calculate and what to track.

## Time computation

### Alabama state court — Ala. R. Civ. P. 6

- **Day of the act is NOT counted.** Day to be done IS counted (last day).
- **Saturdays, Sundays, legal holidays** at the end of a period extend to the next business day.
- **Periods under 7 days** — exclude intermediate Saturdays/Sundays/legal holidays.
- **Periods of 7 days or more** — include intermediate weekends/holidays.
- **Service by mail** — add **3 days** (Ala. R. Civ. P. 6(e)).
- **Service in person, by fax, or by email at a designated address** — no added days.

### Federal court — FRCP 6

- Day of the trigger NOT counted; last day IS counted.
- Weekends/legal holidays at the end extend to the next business day.
- **All days count** for computation (post-2009 amendments — no more excluding weekends in short periods).
- **Service by mail, or electronic service after 5:00 PM local court time** — add **3 days** (FRCP 6(d)).

## Pre-suit notice deadlines (Alabama)

**Critical.** Many AL claims require pre-suit notice. Missing a notice can be fatal.

| Claim against | Notice deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|

| Workers' comp — oral notice to employer | **5 days** from accident | Ala. Code §25-5-78 |

## Calendaring conventions

- **Every deadline** gets a row in the matter's DEADLINES.md with the date, what's due, the rule citation, and status.
- **Reminder dates**: 60 days, 30 days, 14 days, 7 days, 2 days before the deadline (for hard deadlines like SOL).
- **Multi-step events** (e.g., depositions) get all components calendared — notice, prep deadline, the depo itself, post-depo summary.
- **Verify when a deadline lands on a weekend or holiday** — extend to the next business day per the applicable rule.

## What Claude does automatically

- At intake, captures the SOL date and rule citation in the matter's DEADLINES.md
- Flags any matter approaching SOL within 60 days
- Calculates response deadlines using the correct rule (state vs federal) when a new pleading or discovery hits the matter
- Adds the 3-day mail rule when service was by mail
- Warns when a deadline calculation lands on a weekend/holiday and proposes the next business day
- Maintains the DEADLINES.md table

## What requires attorney judgment

- **Accrual date** for SOL — when did the cause of action arise? Discovery rule? Continuing tort? Tolling? Minor or incapacitated plaintiff?
- Whether a particular pre-suit notice applies (some statutes are debated as to coverage)
- Whether to seek an extension or waive a deadline
- Sufficiency of any notice given
- Strategic timing of motions or filings

## When in doubt

Calendar the **earliest plausible deadline**. Then research. Better to file early than miss.
